# Deferred Item Extension Rules (extract)

> Document owner: Engineering · Technical Services　|　ZV-ENG-P-0211　|　Revision 3.1

## 1. Categories and rectification intervals

Deferred defects are classified under the Minimum Equipment List (MEL) into four
categories. The interval runs from **the day after the defect is raised**:

| Category | Rectification interval | Extension |
|---|---|---|
| A | As specified in the individual MEL item; no general rule | **No extension permitted** |
| B | 3 calendar days | One extension of the same length |
| C | 10 calendar days | One extension of the same length |
| D | 120 calendar days | One extension of the same length |

⚠️ **Category severity and time pressure are two different things.** Category A is
the most restrictive — shortest interval, no extension — but if the part is already
in hand it is the item you need to worry about least. What actually stops a dispatch
is the item where **the part will not arrive and the extension has already been
used**.

## 2. Conditions for an extension

All of the following must be satisfied:

1. The item is Category B, C or D. Category A may not be extended.
2. **The item has not been extended before.** One extension only; no second
   extension.
3. The operational and maintenance conditions attached to the deferral remain
   valid.
4. The extension is signed by authorised personnel and entered in the aircraft
   technical log.

## 3. Judging the parts timeline

Every deferred item has two dates that must be read together:

```
Rectify-by date (including any extension)   <- what the regulation allows
Estimated time of arrival for the part      <- what reality allows
```

Any item whose **ETA falls after the rectify-by date** will interrupt dispatch. One
of the following must be started immediately:

- Borrow the part from another station or through a pooling agreement.
- Raise an AOG shipment.
- If neither is possible, plan to ground the aircraft before the deadline and advise
  Operations so the schedule can be adjusted.

## 4. Prohibited

- Do not push back a rectify-by date because the part is "in transit".
- Do not extend an item that has already been extended.
- Do not stop tracking the part simply because the item is Category D. One hundred
  and twenty days still runs out.
