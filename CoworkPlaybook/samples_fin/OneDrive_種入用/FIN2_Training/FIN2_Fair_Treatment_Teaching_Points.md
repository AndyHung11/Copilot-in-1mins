# 2026 Annual Compliance Training — Teaching Points: Fair Treatment of Customers

Run by: Group Compliance Office ｜ Audience: everyone at Zava Financial Holdings

---

## Why this theme, this year

Fair treatment of customers is not a rule you can finish and tick off. The regulator
assesses it annually and **publishes where firms rank**. What gets assessed is not what
the compliance office wrote in a policy — it is **how front-line colleagues actually
treat customers**.

Which means the outcome of this session does not rest with Compliance. It rests at the
counter and with the field sales force.

---

## Six teaching points

### One: Suitability — affording it is not the same as it being suitable

Suitability turns on three things: risk tolerance, investment experience and financial
circumstances. Failing any one of them makes the product unsuitable. You do not average
them out.

> **At the front line**: the customer's risk profile is conservative, but they insist on
> buying a high-risk product and say they will take responsibility themselves. Does a
> signed waiver make it saleable?

### Two: Disclosure — saying it is not the same as explaining it

The standard is whether **the customer understood**, not whether the document was
handed over. Three things must be raised without being asked: charges, risks, and what
an early surrender actually costs.

> **At the front line**: the front-end charge on an investment-linked policy is on page
> 14 of the product description. The customer signed and never once asked about
> charges. Has it been disclosed?

### Three: Advertising and solicitation — you cannot show only the good half

Promotional material must not exaggerate, must not use past performance to imply future
returns, and must not bury material limitations. **Social posts and messaging-app
messages count too.**

> **At the front line**: a sales agent posts "this one was up 20% last year" from their
> personal social account, with no mention of risk. It is a personal account — is it
> solicitation?

### Four: Remuneration and targets — incentives shape behaviour

Where one product pays materially more than another, selling behaviour bends towards
it, and the customer carries the consequence. The remuneration structure is itself part
of fair treatment.

> **At the front line**: one case short of target at month end, with two products both
> suitable for this customer — one paying well, one not. Which do you put forward?

### Five: Complaints — a complaint is data, not a nuisance

Complaints must be answered within the time limit, recorded, and analysed for cause.
When the same kind of complaint keeps recurring, that is a process problem, not a
customer problem.

> **At the front line**: the same adviser has had two complaints of the same nature in
> three months. Both were "resolved by agreement; customer raised nothing further". Is
> that dealt with?

### Six: Vulnerable customers — the same process does not give the same protection

Elderly customers, customers with disabilities and customers who do not read the local
language get **less** protection from an identical standard process. What they need is
an **additional confirmation step**, not a faster one.

> **At the front line**: a 78-year-old arrives with their adult child. The child does
> all the explaining and all the deciding; the customer signs at the end. Procedurally
> there is no defect. In substance?

---

## Closing: three questions to ask yourself

1. If the customer's family reviewed what I did today, **could I account for it**?
2. Did I skip a confirmation step because the customer asked me to?
3. Last time I explained charges, did I **say them** or did I **check they landed**?
