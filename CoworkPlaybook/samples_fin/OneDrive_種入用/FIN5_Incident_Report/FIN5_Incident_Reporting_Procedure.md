# Major Incident Reporting Procedure

Version: 2026 ｜ Maintained by: Group Compliance Office
Applies to: Zava Financial Holdings and its subsidiaries

---

## Article 1 — Reporting threshold

Any of the following is a major incident and must be reported under this procedure:

1. Systems breached, or data stolen, altered or destroyed.
2. **Customer personal data disclosed**, or at risk of disclosure.
3. Material fraud, staff misconduct or a run on deposits.
4. Premises or systems unavailable, affecting customers at scale.
5. Anything else the Head of Compliance considers material.

> Items 1 and 2 frequently arise together — where systems were breached **and**
> customer data was taken, **both** apply, and the obligations that follow are not the
> same. See Article 4.

## Article 2 — ⭐ When the clock starts

**Every deadline in this procedure runs from the moment of awareness.**

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Time of occurrence | When the incident objectively began |
| **Time of awareness** | When the responsible function **confirmed the incident** |

**The clock starts at the time of awareness, not the time of occurrence.**

> In practice these are hours or days apart. System anomalies are usually handled as
> ordinary faults first, and confirming a breach takes time. A subsidiary's notification
> will typically put the **time of occurrence** at the top, in the most prominent
> position — but that **is not** where the clock starts.

## Article 3 — Reporting to the regulator

| Report | Deadline | Counted from | Method |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Initial** | Within **2 hours** of awareness | Time of awareness | Phone or email; key facts only is acceptable |
| **Full** | Within **72 hours** of awareness | Time of awareness | Written report covering cause, impact and remediation |

Who to report to is determined by the Supervisory Bureau Matrix.
**The four subsidiaries answer to different bureaus. Reporting to the wrong one counts
as not having reported.**

## Article 4 — ⭐ The additional duty where personal data is disclosed

Where an incident involves disclosure of customer personal data, the data subjects
**must be notified in addition to** reporting to the regulator under Article 3.

| | Report to the regulator | Notify the data subjects |
|---|---|---|
| Basis | Financial supervision | Personal data protection |
| **Audience** | The supervisory bureau | **The affected customers themselves** |
| Deadline | Initial 2 hrs / full 72 hrs | Within **72 hours** of awareness |
| Counted from | Time of awareness | Time of awareness |
| Content | What happened, scope of impact, remediation | Which data categories were disclosed, likely impact, what the customer can do, our contact point |

> **These are two independent obligations**: different audiences, different content,
> separately counted deadlines. Discharging one does not discharge the other.
>
> Where the disclosed fields include **national ID numbers, account numbers or
> transaction records**, they can be used for impersonation, so the notification to
> data subjects shall set out recommended precautions.

## Article 5 — What a report must contain

1. Time of occurrence and **time of awareness** (both)
2. What happened and the preliminary cause
3. Scope: how many customers affected, which data fields
4. Immediate containment already taken
5. Remediation and timetable
6. Contact point

## Article 6 — Counting the deadlines

Deadlines run in **hours**. Weekends and overnight hours are not deducted. Where the
2-hour initial deadline runs overnight, it still has to be met.
