# Group Exposure Limits — Single Related Party

Version: 2026 ｜ Maintained by: Group Risk Management
Countersigned by: Group Compliance ｜ Reporting date: 2026-09-19

---

## Article 1 — Why exposure is combined

Group business is written **separately**: the bank lends, the life company invests, the
securities arm lends against collateral. Each controls against its own limit, and none
of them can see what the others have done with the same customer.

But for **one and the same related party**, what the group carries is the **combined**
risk. So beyond each subsidiary's own limit, the group sets a combined limit.

## Article 2 — Eligible capital

Eligible capital this period: **NTD 120,000 mn**

> All amounts in this policy are in **NTD millions**.

## Article 3 — The group combined limit

Combined group exposure to a **single related party** shall not exceed
**15%** of eligible capital.

```
group combined limit = 120,000 × 15% = NTD 18,000 mn
```

## Article 4 — Each subsidiary's own limit

| Subsidiary | Exposure type | Own limit |
|---|---|---|
| Zava Commercial Bank | Credit balances | NTD 14,000 mn |
| Zava Life | Investment positions (corporate bonds, equity) | NTD 6,000 mn |
| Zava Securities | Secured lending balances | NTD 2,500 mn |

> ⚠️ **Every subsidiary being inside its own limit does not mean the group is inside
> the combined limit.** These are two separate tests.

## Article 5 — Identifying "the same related party"

1. The **affiliation table** maintained by Group Risk Management is the **sole
   authority**.
2. Relationship **may not** be inferred from how similar two account names look:
   - companies within one related party **may have entirely unrelated names**;
   - two companies with **near-identical names** may belong to different related parties.
3. Where an account name is not in the table, ask Risk Management to add it. Do not
   assign it yourself.

## Article 6 — Calculation

```
combined group exposure to a related party
  = bank credit balances across all members of that related party
  + life investment positions across all members
  + securities secured lending across all members
```

Utilisation = combined exposure ÷ 18,000 × 100%

## Article 7 — Where the limit is breached

1. Risk Management shall table the breach at the Risk Management Committee within
   **five business days**.
2. The paper shall state: the amount of the excess, each subsidiary's contribution, and
   a sensitivity analysis showing **whether removing any single line would still leave
   a breach**.
3. The point of the sensitivity analysis is to find a remedy that **actually works**.

> ⚠️ A common misreading: that unwinding the smallest line is the easy fix. But the
> excess may be **larger** than the smallest line — in which case removing it still
> leaves a breach, and the work achieves nothing.
>
> The sensitivity analysis answers which removals genuinely bring exposure back inside
> the limit. Choose the cheapest to execute from among those that **work**. There may be
> more than one, and it is **not necessarily the smallest**.
