# Supplier Renewal Assessment Principles and Working Rules

> Fictional training sample; suppliers and names are placeholders.

## 1. Renewal lead-time rules

| Annual purchase value | Start renewal work | Renewal review meeting required |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Above NT$10M | **90 days** before expiry | Yes (procurement manager must attend) |
| NT$3M - NT$10M | **60 days** before expiry | Yes |
| Below NT$3M | **30 days** before expiry | No; written sign-off by the responsible buyer |

> Calculate remaining days against today's date of 2026/08/20.

## 2. Assessment criteria (each must be discussed in the meeting)

1. **Supply reliability**: stock-out incidents over the past year, on-time delivery rate.
2. **Quality performance**: incoming-batch rejection rate, and complaints attributable to this supplier.
3. **Price competitiveness**: versus comparable suppliers in the market, and whether negotiating room exists.
4. **Responsiveness**: campaign stock build, gift supply, and speed of response to rush orders.
5. **Risk**: single-source dependency, availability of alternatives, and capacity concentration.

## 3. Meeting scheduling rules

- Duration: 60 minutes for contracts above NT$10M annual value, 30 minutes otherwise.
- Required attendees: the category buyer and the procurement manager; add an operations representative for logistics
  contracts.
- Schedule the meeting after the "start renewal work" date but at least 30 days before expiry - never at the deadline.
- Where one buyer holds several contracts expiring within 30 days of each other, they may be combined into one meeting.

## 4. What the reminder email to each buyer must contain

1. The list of contracts that buyer owns (supplier, contract number, expiry date, days remaining).
2. Whether the "start renewal work" date from the table above has already passed (flag it explicitly if so).
3. The assessment material to prepare, mapped to the five criteria in section 2.
4. The scheduled renewal review meeting time, if one has been booked.

The tone is an internal work reminder between colleagues: direct and specific, without lengthy pleasantries, but not
written as commands.
