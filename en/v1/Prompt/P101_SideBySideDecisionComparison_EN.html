⚖️ Side-by-Side Decision Comparison
✨ Four dimensions side by side → every point marked evidence or assumption → names the two facts you're still missing ⚖️
Build or buy? Migrate now or hold out another year? What stalls these decisions usually isn't your judgement — it's that the information is scattered across a dozen places. The quote is in an email. The concern is buried in one Teams thread. The timeline pressure came up in last month's meeting notes. The technical assessment lives in a document nobody remembers.

So the first thing you do is “let me just build a quick comparison table,” and two hours later the table is half filled in.

This prompt hands that assembly work over. It pulls the relevant material from your emails, chats, meetings, and documents, and sets the two options side by side across cost, risk, effort, and impact on the team or project.

What separates it from a generic “compare A and B for me” is the three demands that follow. First, every point must declare its provenance: is this backed by something concrete in the files, or is it the model's assumption? That single instruction shuts the door on confident-sounding hallucination. Second, name the two pieces of information still missing and where they'd most likely be found — effectively a homework list. Third, no recommendation yet. Its job is to flatten out the picture; the call stays yours.

That last point matters more than it looks. See a conclusion too early and you'll start reasoning backward from it. Get the full picture first, then decide.
  📋 Prompt Template (English)
⚖️ Prompt of the Week (turn on WorkIQ first, then paste into Copilot Chat — Work)

I need to decide between [Option A] and [Option B]. Use everything in my emails, chats, meetings and documents about this topic. Compare both options side by side across: cost, risk, effort, and impact on [team/project]. For each point, tell me whether it's backed by something concrete in our files or whether it's your assumption. Name the two pieces of information I'm still missing to make a clean decision — and where I'd most likely find them. Don't give me a recommendation yet. I want the picture first.

Try it in: Copilot Chat – Work.
  📋 提示詞範本（繁體中文版）
⚖️ 本週 Prompt（先開啟 WorkIQ，再貼進 Copilot Chat – Work）

我必須在 [選項 A] 與 [選項 B] 之間做出決定。請運用我的信件、聊天、會議與文件中所有與這個主題相關的內容。將兩個選項並排比較以下面向：成本、風險、投入，以及對 [團隊／專案] 的影響。針對每一點，請告訴我它是有我們檔案中的具體內容作為依據，還是你的假設。指出我還缺少哪兩項資訊才能做出乾淨俐落的決定——以及我最可能在哪裡找到它們。先不要給我建議。我想先看到全貌。

可在：Copilot Chat – Work 中使用。
💡 Usage Tips
1. 🔓 Turn on WorkIQ first — This prompt reads organizational data (emails, chats, meetings, documents), so WorkIQ (formerly Work mode) must be enabled in Copilot Chat. Without it you'll get a generic comparison with nothing behind it.
2. 🏷️ Make the two options concrete — Don't write “Option 1 / Option 2.” Write “renew with Vendor A” and “migrate to System B.” The closer the wording is to what actually appears in your files, the more evidence it can find.
3. 🚩 Read the assumptions first — Anything labeled an assumption marks a hole in your information. Combined with the two missing facts it names, that's your homework list for the next step.
