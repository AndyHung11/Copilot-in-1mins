🐴 Guardrails for Your AI Agents
✨ Start small → ask for a plan → set red lines → give a finish line → check before risk 🐴
A couple weeks ago, plenty of people were talking about the OpenAI Hugging Face incident, where an AI agent broke into another company's systems. Dramatic stuff. But for most of us, the real everyday risk is way more boring: an email sent too early, files shuffled somewhere weird, something changed before we were ready.

Instead of worrying about agents "going rogue," the fix is setting boundaries the moment you hand off a task. Here are five easy patterns: start small, ask for a plan before it acts, spell out what NOT to do, give it a clear finish line, and require a check before anything hard to undo or shared with others.

Bake these five lines into how you brief your agents, and they behave like a well-mannered assistant instead of an overachiever running wild — you stay in control, and the agent still gets the job done.

🐴 AI Tip of the Week (Eva Etchells, Viva Engage)

Is your agent a rogue one?

A couple weeks ago, a lot of people were talking about the OpenAI Hugging Face incident where an AI agent broke into another company's systems. Drama! But for most of us, the real worry is way more boring: an email sent too early, files shuffled somewhere weird, something changed before we were ready.

So here are five easy ways to guide your agents before they go full overachiever:

1. Start small — Not "Organize all my files." Try: "Organize these 10 files" or "Show me how you'd organize them before you move anything."
2. Ask for a plan — Try: "Before making changes, summarize your plan and wait for my OK." Perfect for sending emails, doc edits, permissions, file moves, etc.
3. Say what NOT to do — Try: "Draft the email, but don't send it." "Find duplicate files, but don't delete anything." "Suggest changes to the document, but don't rewrite the whole thing."
4. Give it a finish line — Agents may keep going with open-ended tasks. Try: "Review the first 20 rows and summarize." Or: "Check these three folders only." Or: "Make one recommendation, then stop."
5. Require a check before anything hard to undo or shared with other people — Try: "If this involves emailing someone, changing a shared file, deleting content, or moving data, stop and ask me first."

Try it in: Copilot Chat (works with any agent or task-based prompt).

🐴 本週 AI 小撇步（Eva Etchells，Viva Engage）

你的代理人是不是有點失控？

前陣子很多人在討論 OpenAI 的 Hugging Face 事件——一個 AI 代理人闖進了別家公司的系統。很戲劇化！但對大多數人來說，真正該擔心的其實樸實無華得多：一封信提早寄出去、幾個檔案被搬到奇怪的地方、東西還沒等你點頭就先被改掉了。

所以這裡有五招簡單的方法，能在代理人「用力過頭」之前先幫它畫好邊界：

1. 從小任務開始 — 不要說「整理我所有的檔案」，試試「整理這 10 個檔案」，或「先跟我說你打算怎麼整理，動手前讓我看一下」。
2. 先要計畫再動手 — 試試「動手改之前，先摘要你的計畫，等我說 OK」。特別適合寄信、改文件、調權限、搬檔案這類任務。
3. 明講不可以做什麼 — 試試「草擬信件，但先不要寄出」「找出重複檔案，但不要刪除任何東西」「建議文件該怎麼改，但不要整篇重寫」。
4. 給它一個終點 — 開放式任務容易讓代理人一路做下去。試試「看前 20 列並摘要」，或「只檢查這三個資料夾」，或「給一個建議就停」。
5. 遇到難以復原或牽涉他人的事，先暫停問過你 — 試試「如果這牽涉到寄信給別人、改動共用檔案、刪除內容或搬動資料，先停下來問我」。

可在：Copilot Chat（適用於任何代理人或任務型提示）中使用。
💡 Usage Tips
1. 🧷 Set it once, reuse it — Save these five lines as your go-to opener and apply them to every task you hand off.
2. 🚦 The riskier the task, the more it needs a check — Always add 'ask me first' for anything involving emails, deletions, or shared files.
3. 🪞 Works beyond Copilot too — These guardrails apply to any agent-based AI, not just Copilot.
