⚖️ Side-by-Side Decision Comparison
Give it two options and it works through your emails, chats, meetings, and documents to compare them side by side across cost, risk, effort, and impact. The crucial part: every point is labeled either “this is actually written somewhere in your files” or “this is my assumption,” and it names the two pieces of information you're still missing plus where you'd most likely find them. And it is explicitly told to hold the recommendation.

This prompt gathers evidence from your emails, chats, meetings, and documents, compares both options across cost, risk, effort, and impact, labels every point as evidence or assumption, names the two facts still missing and where to find them — and deliberately withholds the recommendation.
🎁 What will you get?
🔎 Evidence From Your Own Data
Works through emails, chats, meetings, and documents rather than inventing a comparison table.
📊 Four Dimensions, Side by Side
Cost, risk, effort, and the real impact on your team or project.
🏷️ Evidence vs Assumption, Per Point
Every claim declares its provenance, which shuts out confident-sounding hallucination.
🚧 Deliberately No Recommendation
It flattens the picture and names the two missing facts; the judgement stays with you.
🚀 Copy This Prompt (English Version)
⚖️ Prompt of the Week (turn on WorkIQ first, then paste into Copilot Chat — Work)

I need to decide between [Option A] and [Option B]. Use everything in my emails, chats, meetings and documents about this topic. Compare both options side by side across: cost, risk, effort, and impact on [team/project]. For each point, tell me whether it's backed by something concrete in our files or whether it's your assumption. Name the two pieces of information I'm still missing to make a clean decision — and where I'd most likely find them. Don't give me a recommendation yet. I want the picture first.

Try it in: Copilot Chat – Work.

可在：Copilot Chat – Work 中使用。
💡 Three Ways to Use It
1
🔓 Turn on WorkIQ first
This prompt reads organizational data (emails, chats, meetings, documents), so WorkIQ (formerly Work mode) must be enabled in Copilot Chat. Without it you'll get a generic comparison with nothing behind it.
2
🏷️ Make the two options concrete
Don't write “Option 1 / Option 2.” Write “renew with Vendor A” and “migrate to System B.” The closer the wording is to what actually appears in your files, the more evidence it can find.
3
🚩 Read the assumptions first
Anything labeled an assumption marks a hole in your information. Combined with the two missing facts it names, that's your homework list for the next step.
